# F217 A QR vault is a third form of a seed in the backup, with Open to test before the lock (2026-11-05)

**Kind:** History
**Status:** Withdrawn
**Area:** backup

*Withdrawn (owner, 2026-11-05): a QR vault does not go in the backup flow, since nobody would use it; nothing of it was built. Its amendments to F189, F31 and F103 are gone with it. What stays from it, in `docs/VAULT.md` §2.1: Faraday Starter does not scan or print a QR vault, and its paper is the device's labelled PNG.*

**Why.** The owner's ask: a backup could keep a seed as a QR vault on
paper in place of its words. Under the question on vaults a QR vault
was either its own box or a vault on a stick, and neither fits a code
that holds one thing, needs no stick and is not a secret. The lock wipes
a new seed once its copies are made, and a code's paper exists only after
the lock, so a code whose passphrase never opened it would be the seed's
only copy.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-05; withdrawn the same day, with the section of
`docs/BACKUP-FLOW.md` that held the spec).

1. On the backup board a QR vault is a third form on a seed, a codex32
   string or a passphrase, beside Words and SeedQR, not its own box.
2. "Will you use vaults as part of this backup?" has three answers: No,
   Yes on sticks, Yes as QR codes. The last brings in no stick before
   the keys and places each seed as a QR vault on paper in place of its
   words, and a passphrase as its own QR vault at its own place.
3. Faraday Starter does not scan or print a QR vault. Its paper is the
   device's labelled PNG, carried on a stick to a computer with a
   printer, which may be online since the code is not a secret.
4. A code need not be scanned back. Scan to check compares bytes and
   needs no passphrase, and until it is done the item is done and reads
   "Not scanned back" as a warning.
5. A QR vault's passphrase has a warning line and no minimum beyond not
   being empty.
6. Under Yes as QR codes the lock wipes a new seed before its code is on
   paper, so the code's `.pgp` file goes on the stick beside its PNG, and
   the flow offers **Open to test** before the lock: the passphrase typed
   to open the code and its contents compared with what the plan put in.
   Skipping it is a warning, not a refusal. It proves that the passphrase
   opens the bytes that go on the stick; Scan to check stays as it is and
   proves the print. The passphrase typed is wiped as the flow's other
   uses are, and the test costs one Argon2id run.
