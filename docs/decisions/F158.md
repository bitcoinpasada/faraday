# F158 Faraday's own constructions in the app until an outside review: cards, the camera, a mix, the vault's container and the Secure Boot generator (2026-10-28)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Amended by F160
**Area:** security
**Held by:** standards.what-goes-in, crypto.tested

*Amended by F160 (2026-10-29): the vault area checks the container against a model Faraday's authors wrote, over argon2-cffi and libsodium for the primitives only; each construction here carries the label "own construction, not yet independently reviewed" until an outside review.*

**Why.** STANDARDS §0 admits a cryptographic construction of Faraday's
own only once it has been reviewed, or when it can only add to a
standard's strength. A review of the constructions without a decision
(STANDARDS §7) found five in the app on neither ground. Each is kept,
changed or limited here, until the outside review of §7 reaches it.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-28).

1. **Cards stay a key's source.** The mapping is Faraday's own: a card
   is the index `suit × 13 + rank` (suits ♠ ♥ ♦ ♣, ranks A to K), and
   the entropy is SHA-256 of those indices, one byte each, in the order
   drawn, truncated to the strength (`core/faraday-entropy/src/cards.rs`).
   No other wallet's card mode is claimed to give the same key. The bits
   counted (log2 of the cards left in the deck, per draw) assume a
   well-shuffled deck, about seven riffle shuffles; a deck shuffled less
   carries fewer. New key cautions, as it does for a run of dice counting
   up, when five cards in a row are each the one after (or each the one
   before) the last in the deck's own order, as a deck out of its box
   comes (`CARDS_IN_ORDER`, "Cards in the order of a new deck"). It is a
   caution, not a refusal, as the dice cautions are.
   *Why kept:* the extraction is the published dice procedure's (SHA-256
   of a written record), the count is exact for a uniform permutation,
   and a key can be worked out again by hand from the cards written
   down; a poor shuffle is the same kind of risk a biased die is.
2. **The camera only adds.** Camera frames add to the system's
   randomness (Add randomness, New key's This device through
   `device_own`, F86) or to a mix as one member. They are never a key's
   only source: New key lists no Camera row (`SOURCE_ROWS`, `WAYS`), and
   `KeyGen::finish_entry` makes no key from the camera alone. A frame
   whose SHA-256 was already taken is refused ("That picture was already
   taken").
   *Why:* nothing estimates how much noise survives a camera's processing
   and the downscaling; the source trusts the shell, the driver and the
   firmware, so a virtual or stalled camera gives a fixed key that passes
   every check; and nobody can check the result by hand.
3. **A mix needs dice or this device.** A mix makes a key only with dice
   or this device's generator among its two to five sources
   (`KeyGen::mix_enough`; otherwise "A mix takes dice or this device
   among its sources"). Every member is entered to its own full count,
   so the mix holds the whole strength of the published dice procedure
   (Coldcard's, SeedSigner's) or of the system's generator, and the
   other members can only add (SHA-256 over the members' 32-byte
   commitments, in the order taken). A mixed key is not a standard's
   procedure, so another wallet does not make it again.
   *Why:* under STANDARDS §0 a mix then only adds to a standard's
   strength; a mix of only Faraday's own sources (cards, coins, the
   camera) would be only as strong as the best of them.
4. **The vault's four-slot container stays, Faraday's own until an
   outside review.** Its parts are published standards (Argon2id, RFC
   9106; HKDF-SHA256, RFC 5869; XChaCha20-Poly1305,
   draft-irtf-cfrg-xchacha), composed as the usual password-based AEAD,
   and the F79 area `vault` checks it against libsodium and argon2-cffi.
   Faraday's own: the four equal slots, unused slots filled from a
   keystream, the one salt for every slot, and the seal nonce. It is
   first in line for the outside review (STANDARDS §7).
   - **The seal nonce** is the first 24 bytes of a fresh system answer
     for "vault seal" (`Faraday::fresh`) when the pool holds one, and
     only when none waits the session's seed with a count, as before
     (`Faraday::vault_seal_nonce`, `docs/VAULT.md` §5). XChaCha20's
     192-bit nonce is made for a random nonce per encryption, and a fresh
     answer also covers a process brought back with the same seed (a
     virtual machine restored from a snapshot); the fallback keeps a
     lock from waiting on the system. The file format is unchanged.
   - **Rollback** is outside what the slots promise: an older copy of a
     slot still opens (`docs/VAULT.md` §9).
   *Why kept:* compiling it out would remove the place keys are kept, for
   a container with no known flaw; its new claim (deniability) fails
   toward losing deniability, not confidentiality.
5. **The Secure Boot generator stays.** RSA key generation, the owner
   GUID, the serials and the signatures' blinding draw from ChaCha20
   (RFC 8439: 96-bit nonce, 32-bit block counter from 0) used as a
   stream, keyed directly by a 32-byte fresh system answer and with the
   purpose in the nonce's first byte, the rest zero
   (`faraday/faraday-sb/src/rng.rs`, `Stream`), as libsodium's
   `randombytes_buf_deterministic` and the Linux kernel's generator use
   ChaCha20. It is checked against RFC 8439 Appendix A.1's keystream and
   Python `cryptography`'s ChaCha20, and every private key it makes
   passes `openssl pkey -check` (`faraday/faraday-sb/tests/judged.rs`).
   *Why kept:* it is a textbook generator over the same `chacha20` crate
   the vault uses, a fixed expansion of a 256-bit seed for 112-bit
   RSA-2048 that cannot add strength and is now checked outside Faraday;
   `rand_chacha` would add a dependency and change every golden hash for
   nothing.
