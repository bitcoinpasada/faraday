# F81 Modes: a pressable pill, Practice on testnet, samples at every step (2026-10-22)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Amended by F119
**Area:** design

*Amended by F119 (2026-10-26): the mode is named Practice, not Demo; the code keeps `Mode::Demo`.*

**Why.** The network pill was a badge that opened nothing, and the
chooser sat in Settings (SIMPLIFY §1.5). Faraday Demo's mainnet was
its demo (F20) and the website's demo was mainnet too, so "demo" meant
mainnet with public seeds: an address of a public dummy wallet was a
mainnet address, kept from use only by a broken checksum. Moving a
session between networks re-derived its keys in place, so demo keys and
real keys could sit in one session, and the rules for when it could move
grew refusals. A person trying Faraday had to find the eleven public
seeds' words before any flow could go on. The spec is `docs/MODES.md`.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-22).

- **The pill opens the mode sheet.** The network pill is pressable
  wherever it shows, which is every mode but Mainnet, on the desktop,
  the stick and the Pi, in the wide and the small-panel layouts. It
  opens a sheet listing the modes. Settings keeps a **Mode** row that
  opens the same sheet, the only door on Mainnet, where no pill shows. A
  mode that cannot be chosen is dimmed with its label alone, no reason
  line (DESIGN §4.11: nothing on the device changes it).
- **Modes.** Faraday Demo: Practice, Testnet, Signet, Regtest; it has no
  Mainnet. The stick and the Pi: Mainnet, Testnet, Signet, Regtest; no
  Practice, no Load samples. The website: Practice only, the other three listed
  and dimmed.
- **Practice is testnet.** Practice's keys and wallets are testnet-shaped (tb1
  addresses, coin type 1), on the desktop and the website alike, so no
  bitcoin can be sent to a public dummy wallet. In code, Practice is the
  key store's set of public keys (`Keys::set_demo`) on Testnet:
  `faraday-keys` stays the only place that knows a key is public, and
  `Faraday::makes_secrets` and DEMO §4's table key off Practice, not off
  mainnet. Practice shows real testnet addresses; the broken checksum goes.
  The pill says so: it reads "Practice · Testnet", and the mode sheet's
  Practice row carries "Testnet" as its value.
- **A mode change is a lock.** Choosing another mode locks, as the Lock
  action does (with its LockAsk and what it keeps), and the fresh session
  starts in the mode chosen, so demo keys and real keys never meet. A
  lock keeps the mode the person was in. A mode change also empties
  Files, From the stick and For the stick, and carries nothing but the
  mode; a plain lock keeps the boxes (owner, 2026-10-22). Nothing is dimmed for "wallets
  loaded": `Session::set_network`'s in-place move and its refusals go
  from every path a person can take. A wallet or a transaction from
  another network moves the session only when it holds no key and no
  wallet, is not in Practice, and the mode is one this app offers;
  otherwise it is refused, naming its network.
- **The desktop starts in Practice**, developer, test and release builds
  alike.
- **No online warning sheet.** F61's warning sheet goes: a person can
  type a seed on an online computer in any mode, Practice included, so a
  sheet on one path protects nothing. Instead, on the desktop and the
  website, every place a seed or a secret can be typed carries one
  line under its field, "Never type a seed that holds real bitcoin
  here.", in every mode. The stick and the Pi, offline, do not. The website sets the
  seed line (`Faraday::seed_line`) without becoming Faraday Demo (owner,
  2026-10-22).
- **Samples.** In Practice a second pill, **Load samples**, opens a sheet of
  tick boxes (F62's Load sheet is the model): the sample wallets, each
  loading with its seeds, and the sample keys, one or more of every form
  Faraday reads a key in (`docs/MODES.md` §5), each from a published
  vector where one exists; a key that takes a passphrase is public
  with none and with `faraday`. A set of parts (SLIP-39 2 of 3, codex32
  shares, Seed XOR parts) is one tick that loads the key its threshold
  makes. Ticking does not follow New key's handout order; the handout
  takes the next sample of its length not loaded.
- **Use a sample at every step.** In Practice, wherever a flow asks for a
  key or part of one, a wallet, a transaction or a vault, the empty
  field holds a **Use a sample** button where a placeholder would be (on
  the scanner, a **Sample** tile). It fills the field with the matching
  sample of that exact form, as if typed or scanned: the next one
  unused, and for a part, the next part of the set begun. It goes as
  soon as the person presses in the field and types, so a field can be
  tried by hand. A secret a sample filled is shown unmasked: samples are
  public. Where a flow asks for the same value again, the button fills
  the matching value; what the person typed is theirs to remember.
- **Every passphrase field offers `faraday`** (owner, 2026-10-22). In
  Practice every passphrase and password field is typed, masked and edited as
  outside Practice, with its eye and its caution line; an empty one also
  offers a one-press chip, "faraday", inside it, like Use a sample's. The
  chip goes when the person presses in the field or types. A press on it
  puts `faraday` (all lowercase) in the field, unmasked in a code box so
  its exact characters are plain; a press in the field then lets the
  person clear or replace it, and what they type is theirs. A second
  field that types a passphrase back, and an unlock, offer the chip too,
  so `faraday` is always one press away. An optional passphrase stays
  optional. Generate with dice still shows the rolled words and puts
  `faraday` in their place. The KeePass export's passphrase is typed by
  the person in Practice, its 8-character minimum unchanged, and has no chip,
  since `faraday` is under the minimum. `demo::PASSPHRASE` becomes
  `faraday`. The Load samples sheet and the Learn page say once what it
  is; no field explains it. Faraday refuses no passphrase for being short
  or weak (only an empty one, or a repeat that differs), so nothing is
  loosened; outside Practice every field is as before and has no chip. No
  permanent passphrase pill; the Load samples sheet lists no "in use"
  values.
- **Each sample says what it is.** Every row of the Load samples sheet
  carries its type: a wallet its kind and script ("2-of-3 multisig ·
  native SegWit", "single-sig · Taproot"), a key its form and size
  ("BIP-39 · 24 words", "SLIP-39 · 2 of 3 shares").
- **New key in Practice** still runs every source, and at Lock in its result
  is replaced by a sample of the length chosen (12-word samples are
  added: twelve, ocean, piano, silver; and 15, 18 and 21-word ones),
  stated in one line on the Key card, as DEMO §5.1 has it. A passphrase
  chosen there is `faraday`.
- **The samples are public data in their own crate**, faraday-samples:
  words, shares, descriptors, transactions and fingerprints as literals,
  loaded through the same paths a typed or scanned key takes. A test
  checks every literal against testkit or its published vector. Only the
  desktop app and the website depend on it; a lint fails if the stick,
  the Pi, faraday-disk, faraday-grant, faraday-boot or the Starter
  reaches it. The testkit stays out of every shipped binary (F70).
- **The website** starts in Practice, its mode fixed, and moves off
  faraday-core's `testkit` feature to faraday-samples. Its three stories
  stop building wallets and seeds of their own: Bring in a wallet and
  Spend using a Faraday backup use the sample Savings (2 of 3), its seed
  papers the sample's three seeds and its phone the sample's unsigned
  spend, on testnet; Start fresh makes its own wallet from the key New
  key hands out, as it does today. The website's change is a batch in
  its own repository.
- **Amends** F20 (Faraday Demo's mainnet is no longer the demo: Practice
  is a mode on testnet, its addresses are real, and the mainnet refusal
  goes), F30 (the demo is the Practice mode, not a demo session on mainnet),
  F61 (the desktop starts in Practice; the online warning goes), DEMO §2's
  definition of the demo, §5.2's public passphrase (now
  `faraday`) and §6's rows for the LND cipher seed (open in Practice,
  taking only LND's published vector) and for an entry's password (now
  `faraday`, not typed), and SIMPLIFY
  §1.5's badge that opens nothing.
