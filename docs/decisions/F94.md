# F94 Codex32 error correction, opt-in (2026-10-22)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** crypto
**Held by:** tests

**Why.** `faraday_bip::codex32` said it detects and does not correct: a
string whose checksum fails is refused and nothing is offered in its
place. BIP 93 says implementations should offer corrections and should
not proceed without confirmation, and the Codex32QR profile's author
built such a flow on SeedSigner. A worn or misread share is the case a
backup in shares is most likely to meet. The plan is
`docs/CODEX32.md` §8.4.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-22).

- The codex32 form and Check a string carry a tick, **Suggest
  corrections**, off whenever the form opens. Off, a failing string is
  refused as before.
- On, a failing string is decoded within BIP 93's bound: up to 4
  substitutions, or up to 8 unreadable boxes marked `?`, or any mix with
  twice the substitutions plus the unreadable boxes at most 8; and a run
  of 9 to 13 unreadable boxes in a row, with its own caution. At every
  length. `ms1` is never changed; insertions, deletions and swapped
  groups are not searched for.
- A proposal is shown only where it is unique within that bound, and
  never applied: the person types the changed boxes again from the
  paper, and only an exact match is taken; a repair to damaged paper is
  marked "Repaired, not checked", and the fingerprint where the key
  loads carries a line saying the entry was corrected.
- The decoder only proposes: everything it returns is parsed again by
  `Codex32::parse`, which stays the only way in. It is tested against
  python-codex32's frozen correction corpus and, through the crypto
  cross-check (F79), against its correction code.
- This replaces the codec's statement that it "detects; it does not
  correct" once the decoder is built.
