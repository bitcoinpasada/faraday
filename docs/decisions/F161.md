# F161 A finalized input carries the smallest valid witness by its real size, a tie broken as Bitcoin Core breaks it (2026-10-29)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** crypto
**Held by:** tests

**Why.** A PSBT input's signatures can satisfy its script in more than
one way. `miniscript` 12 chooses by an estimate that counts every ECDSA
signature as 73 bytes, though a DER signature with its sighash byte is
71 to 73, so it can write a witness a byte or two heavier than another
it could have written. The cross-check against Bitcoin Core's
`finalizepsbt` (F160) found Faraday writing a `multi()` inside a
miniscript with signatures that were not the smallest, and, where two
satisfactions weigh the same, choosing another than Core's. Two
finalizers that agree byte for byte make the same transaction from the
same PSBT, so a coordinator and the device give one txid.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-29).

1. Faraday finalizes a PSBT input with the smallest valid witness by
   its real serialized size: every element with its length, the
   signatures as they are on the PSBT.
2. On equal size the tie is broken as Bitcoin Core v31.1 breaks it:
   - `or_b`, `or_c`, `or_d`, `or_i`: the left branch; `andor`: its
     `and`;
   - `multi()`: of signatures of one length, those of the keys named
     first;
   - `thresh()`: the later branches satisfied, the earlier
     dissatisfied;
   - a taproot script path: the leaf whose script sorts first by its
     bytes, then by leaf version, under its shortest control block.
3. A plain multisig (`sh(multi)`, `wsh(multi)`, `sh(wsh(multi))`) takes
   its first m signatures in key order, as Core's own template does.
4. What can be finalized at all is unchanged: every witness so chosen
   is checked by `miniscript`'s interpreter, and nothing refused before
   is accepted.

The rule is in `core/faraday-psbt/src/finalize.rs` and
`core/faraday-psbt/src/finalize/smallest.rs`; the finalizer vectors in
`tools/vectors/psbt/` are Core's answers, and
`core/faraday-psbt/tests/all/satisfaction.rs` holds them.
