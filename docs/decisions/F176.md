# F176 Anti-exfil names two levels, checked signature and protected key; Faraday as host keeps the second, with rho in memory only and aborts counted in the vault; the reveal never in a PSBT (2026-10-30)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** crypto
**Held by:** standards.experimental-dev-only

**Why.** FractalEncrypt answered the review of its air-gapped exchange
(2026-10-30). It agreed that a fallback to ordinary signing and a fresh
`rho` after an interruption weaken what can be claimed for a key, and
asked how the draft standard should separate the protection a single
checked signature has from the protection of a key across its use. Its
answer to the review's carriage also found a contradiction in it: the
review asked that `rho` stay out of PSBTs and also that it travel in a
registered PSBT field. A PSBT that carries `rho` for one stage is saved,
backed up, combined and logged by ordinary PSBT tools before anything
can strip it. F162 left two questions open: the abort count past which
the screen says to move the funds, and the stage 2 review on Faraday as
signer.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-30). `docs/ANTI-EXFIL.md` is the spec.

1. **Two levels** (§2.1). A *checked signature*: both checks passed for
   this signature, and nothing more is claimed. A *protected key*: the
   host refuses every signature by the key without a passed exchange at
   every way out, retries after the reveal with the same `rho` or counts
   the abort, counts aborts after the reveal for the key's life where a
   restart does not lose them, and makes turning protection off a
   deliberate, recorded step. A checked signature is said to be
   "checked"; only a protected key is "protected".
2. **Enforced before resuming exists.** The protected-key refusals hold
   from the start; an exchange that cannot be finished leaves the
   payment unsigned, and a new transaction is a new, counted exchange.
3. **Faraday as host keeps a protected key**, with `rho` in memory only.
   It is not derived from a kept secret so that a restart could rebuild
   it: that secret would rebuild every past `rho`, and so every key a
   backdoored signer hid in a checked signature, wherever the vault is
   copied. An offline host rarely loses an exchange after the reveal (a
   failed scan is scanned again with the same `rho`); a lost one costs
   a new transaction and one counted abort.
4. **A protected exchange needs an open vault**, which counts aborts
   after the reveal per signer key for the key's life (the transaction,
   the fingerprint, each slot's `c` and `R1`, never `rho`). An abort
   before the reveal is not counted. A warning from the first abort;
   past 20 (libsecp256k1-zkp's header) the screen says to move the funds
   to a new key. A copy of the vault from before an abort holds a lower
   count, which the device cannot detect; the spec says so.
5. **No review at stage 2** on Faraday as signer. One screen says that
   nothing is signed and shows the transaction's fingerprint; the review
   and the approval are at stage 4.
6. **The reveal is never in a PSBT.** For the draft BIP Faraday proposes
   the semantics, the two levels, the host rules as MUSTs and shared
   negative vectors first; then a split carriage: `c`, `R1` and the
   signatures in BIP-registered PSBT input fields, and `rho` in a
   message of its own type, bound to the PSBT by the unsigned
   transaction's id and the session id. The detached and in-PSBT forms
   are weighed side by side until the draft settles it. Faraday offers a
   second implementation from another codebase, its btclib checks of
   FractalEncrypt's vectors, negative vectors and review of the
   normative text.

F162's dev-build rule is unchanged: none of this is in a shipped app
until a published standard carries it.
