# F286 Each key of a wallet of several keys carries a check: yours, checked by you or not checked, and the descriptor checksum is not proof (2026-11-17)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** wallets
**Held by:** review

*Amends F254. Built: all seven points. The key block, its four states and the line under it are in `faraday/faraday-core/src/wallet/confirm.rs` and `faraday/faraday-core/src/wallet/keycheck.rs`; a wallet's states are kept on it and worked out in `faraday/faraday-core/src/wallet.rs` when it is added and when a key is loaded or removed (`Session::drop_forged`); the chart node's line, the wallet sheet, the receive caution and the review's block draw from them; Check a multisig setup is `faraday/faraday-core/src/multisig_check/mod.rs`; the vault's field is `faraday.checked` and field 4 of the wallet record (`faraday/faraday-vault/src/records.rs`, `faraday/faraday-vault/src/kdbx/items/`).*

**Why.** F254's sheet shows every key of a wallet of several keys and
says to compare each with its own device, but nothing shows which keys
the device could check itself, and nothing records that the person
compared the rest. The eight-character descriptor checksum under the keys
is the easy thing to look at, and it can be forged: it is 40 bits, a
typing check, and a different wallet with the same eight characters can be
made in seconds to hours. Malware on the online computer can swap in a
wallet with its own keys in place of the cosigners', with the same name,
shape, fingerprints, paths and checksum; the person holds to add; Faraday
then shows that wallet's receive addresses as theirs. Only a key compared
whole with its own device, or one Faraday derived from a loaded seed,
rules that out.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-17; `docs/WALLETS.md` §3.5,
`docs/FLOWS.md` confirmation sheet, Wallets tab, Spend tab and Check a
multisig setup, `docs/guide/multisig-check.md`):

1. **The key block.** Every place that shows the keys of a wallet of
   several keys draws one block (`faraday/faraday-core/src/wallet/confirm.rs`).
   Each key has its number, fingerprint, path, the whole extended key in
   groups of four, and one of four states: a green check, "Yours, can
   sign" (Faraday derived it from a loaded seed; "Yours, can sign · your
   single-key account" for the single-key account form F254 point 2
   allows; a key made here whose backup is not done reads "Yours, signs
   after its backup", with "· your single-key account" in that form, as it
   signs nothing until its backup is done); a green check, "Checked by you" (the person ticked it); a
   grey circle, "Not checked" (nothing vouches for it; a press ticks it,
   another unticks it; ticking is optional); a red cross (the fingerprint
   is a loaded key's and the key is not that key's). Under the keys one
   line: grey "2 keys not checked · compare each with its own device", or
   green "Every key checked". The descriptor checksum stays the last row,
   in the quieter tone, and is described as a typing check, not proof.
2. **A wallet arrives** through F254's sheet, which shows the block. With
   the seed loaded the person's key is green and the others grey; with
   none loaded all are grey. The hold adds the wallet whether or not
   anything is ticked; the confirmation reads "{name} added" or "{name}
   added · 2 keys not checked".
3. **After it is added** "Not checked" stays with the wallet for the
   session: its node on the chart has a second line, "2 keys not
   checked", in the caution tone; its own sheet shows the block, tickable,
   under the Descriptor row; receive addresses (first addresses, an
   address's code) carry a caution line, "2 keys of this wallet are not
   checked", and are not held back; a spend's review shows the block while
   any key is grey, and one green line "Every key checked" once none is.
4. **A seed loaded later.** Every loaded wallet is checked against it
   again: a matching key turns green by itself and earlier ticks stay. A
   wallet with a key that claims the seed's fingerprint and is not its key
   is taken out of the session with a notice (`Session::drop_forged`), as
   F135 already does.
5. **Check a multisig setup** shows the same block with the same ticks;
   where the setup is a wallet loaded here the marks are that wallet's.
6. **A vault remembers the ticks** in a small field of the wallet's own
   record, which holds the whole descriptor and has no identifier of
   Faraday's own, so that an unchecked wallet saved into a vault does not
   come back looking checked. A wallet loaded from a vault still skips the
   sheet (F254's amendments).
7. **The states are worked out** when a wallet is added and when a key is
   loaded or removed, never while drawing.

**Not changed.** A single-key wallet shows no block; a wallet whose keys
are all the person's is green at once and skips the sheet; a lock ends the
session and the ticks no vault holds; nothing is refused for a key left
unchecked. Not decided here: further ways the device itself turns a grey
key green (an open vault that holds the key; the key scanned from the
cosigner's own screen or the paper backup's code).
