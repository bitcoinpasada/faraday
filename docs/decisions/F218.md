# F218 The list of what was plugged in at start can be dismissed (2026-11-05)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** security
**Held by:** tests

*Amends F202 (2026-11-05): its rule that nothing dismisses the notice of what was plugged in at start; everything else it says holds.*

**Why.** The notice shows for 10 s and takes much of a small screen, and a
person who has read it has to wait for what is under it (owner, 2026-11-05).

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-05).

1. **Its × or Escape.** The notice has a close affordance at its end, as
   Material 3's snackbar allows, a `TOUCH` square in the snackbar's ink.
   Escape from a trusted keyboard also hides it while it shows, for someone
   with no pointer, and does nothing else. Either hides it for this power-on.
   No other key, swipe or press elsewhere does.
2. **Only a person's press counts.** A press before the first frame that
   draws it counts nothing, and a press on the × never reaches what is under
   it. A device still held back cannot dismiss it: its keys and presses
   arrive as events of its own and reach neither path.
3. **Otherwise as F202.** It still shows at every power-on with something to
   list, still goes after `BOOT_NOTICE_MS`, and a toast still stands above it.

**The accepted gap.** F202 kept the notice undismissable since a bad device
could move first and clear it. A malicious device trusted at start can now
press the × or type Escape blind, and a person who looks away for that moment
does not see the list. It already could type or click blind (F202); the
notice is how a person sees it, and that is now weaker against a device built
to clear it. The owner asked for it, for the room on a small screen.
