# F232 A BOLT 12 offer is read in Decode a Lightning invoice, read only, through a reader of Faraday's own, checked against Core Lightning's code (2026-11-10)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active, nothing built
**Area:** formats
**Held by:** review

**Why.** Decode a Lightning invoice (Tools, Transactions; Home's Scan
opens it) reads a BOLT #11 invoice (`core/faraday-codec/src/bolt11.rs`).
More and more payees give an offer instead: one line starting `lno1`
that does not expire with one payment, which a payer's node turns into
an invoice by asking the payee's node over the Lightning network. A
person who is given an offer can see on Faraday what it asks for and
which node it names before paying it from a wallet that is online.
BOLT #12, "Negotiation Protocol for Lightning Payments", defines offers,
invoice requests, invoices, invoice errors and payer proofs. It was
merged into the master branch of `lightning/bolts` on 2024-09-24 and
has been amended there since (the latest change to its text read for
this decision, of 2026-10-19, refuses Unicode control characters in its
strings, by BOLT #1's rule). The repository gives its documents no
status of their own; BOLT #12 is merged, implemented by several node
implementations (Core Lightning, LDK, Eclair) and used by wallets, so
STANDARDS reads it as Deployed, as it reads BOLT #11. It is a published
standard under STANDARDS §0, and this decision says how it enters,
because the reader is a piece written here where an established library
exists (F178).

**Decision** (2026-11-10).

- **Offers only, read only.** Decode a Lightning invoice reads an offer,
  told by its prefix `lno1`, beside an invoice, in the same text field
  and from the same scans (a `lightning:` prefix taken as for an
  invoice). Nothing is paid, no invoice request is made, nothing is
  sent, signed or kept; Faraday is offline and could not send one. An
  invoice request (`lnr1`) is refused with a line saying Faraday reads
  offers, not invoice requests. A BOLT #12 invoice has no text form in
  BOLT #12 (it travels only inside the Lightning network), so a text
  calling itself one (`lni1`, which some tools print) is refused as an
  unknown prefix, and so are invoice errors and payer proofs. An offer
  inside a `bitcoin:` URI (BIP 321's `lno` parameter) is not read by
  this decision. The reason for offers alone: an offer is what a payee
  hands a person; an invoice request and a BOLT #12 invoice are messages
  between two nodes, which a person does not copy by hand.
- **Written here, in `faraday-codec`, beside the BOLT #11 reader.** The
  established implementation is rust-lightning's `lightning` crate, its
  `offers` module. It is not taken: the reason is F178's "only the part
  used is written". The crate is a whole Lightning node, about 220,000
  lines in 0.2.7, of which the offer reader is about 3,000; taking it
  would bring that whole crate into the app for a read view, with
  `lightning-types`, `lightning-invoice`, `dnssec-prover`,
  `possiblyrandom` and a second version of `hashbrown` beside the one
  the app locks, each with its `docs/deps/` page and its `cargo vet`
  audit, and a new trust entry under F71 for a publisher Faraday does
  not trust today. Its `no_std` build (without its `std` feature) would
  serve, so that is not the reason. Faraday's reader is the part used:
  the text form, BOLT #1's TLV stream and its types, the offer's fields
  and the blinded path, over the `bech32` crate's reader without a
  checksum and secp256k1's parse of a point, both libraries the app
  already uses, with no `unsafe`. It is not cryptography (an offer
  carries no signature, and nothing is hashed), so
  `tools/lint-crypto.counts` does not count it, as F229 says of its
  SQLite reader.
- **What the offer view shows**, a row for each field the offer
  carries, as an invoice's view does: that it is an offer; the networks
  it is for (Bitcoin when it names none), each known chain by its name
  and any other by its hash; the amount, in satoshis with any
  millisatoshis when it is in bitcoin, or in a currency's own units
  with its ISO 4217 code, and "Any amount" when it names none; the
  description; the issuer, marked as the issuer's own words; the issuer
  id, a node key; each blinded path, by its first node (a node key, or
  a short channel id and direction) and its number of hops; the expiry,
  as a date and time in UTC; the quantity it allows (any number for 0,
  else the most); the features it sets, by bit; the metadata, by its
  length; and each unknown odd field, by its type and length. The
  expiry is shown and not judged: an offline computer's clock is not
  trusted to say whether an offer has expired. An offer carries no
  signature, so the view says nothing of who made it: the issuer id and
  the paths name a node, to be compared with what the payee gives from
  another source, as for an invoice's key. BOLT #12 defines no offer id
  (Core Lightning and LDK each compute their own, differently), so none
  is shown.
- **Refused, as BOLT #12 and BOLT #1 make them refusals:** mixed case;
  a `+` that does not stand between two Bech32 characters; a TLV
  stream whose types do not strictly increase, whose length runs past
  its end, whose BigSize or truncated integer is not minimal, or whose
  field has the wrong length; a field outside the offer's ranges (1 to
  79, 1,000,000,000 to 1,999,999,999); an unknown even field; a feature
  field not minimally encoded, or with an even bit set that Faraday
  does not know (BOLT #9 defines no offer feature today, so every even
  bit), the bit named; a description, issuer or currency that is not
  BOLT #1's UTF-8 (invalid, or with a character of Unicode's Cc, Cf,
  Cs or Co categories, or a line or paragraph separator); an amount
  without a description, an amount of zero, a currency without an
  amount; neither an issuer id nor a path; a path of no hops; a point
  that is not a valid compressed secp256k1 key; chains present but
  empty, or of a length not a multiple of 32; and, as BOLT #12's vectors
  do, padding of more than four bits.
- **Stricter, where BOLT #12 leaves a case open (fail closed):** a text
  over 4,296 characters, the most a QR code holds in capital letters; a
  non-zero padding bit; whitespace after a `+` other
  than spaces, tabs and line breaks; a chain named twice; chains naming
  no Bitcoin network Faraday knows (mainnet, testnet3, testnet4, signet,
  regtest); a currency code that is not three capital letters or not in
  ISO 4217's list, since its units could not be shown; an amount in
  millisatoshis above 21 million bitcoin; an expiry past the year 9999;
  an `offer_paths` that holds no path; a character in Unicode's Cn (unassigned) category, which BOLT #1
  allows a reader to refuse. A refused offer shows the reason in one
  line, as an invoice does.
- **Checked against BOLT #12's published vectors and Core Lightning's
  code** (F160). The vectors of `bolt12/` in `lightning/bolts`,
  `offers-test.json` (53 offers, valid and not) and
  `format-string-test.json` (the text form and `+`), are vendored at a
  pinned commit under `tools/vectors/` and read in `faraday-codec`'s
  tests; `signature-test.json` and `payer-proof-test.json` are for the
  forms Faraday does not read and are not used. The F79 area `lightning`
  takes, beside its BOLT #11 judge, Core Lightning's own decoder,
  `devtools/bolt12-cli decode` over `common/bolt12.c`, built from a
  pinned commit of Core Lightning as `tools/reference/cln/` pins it:
  code of another lineage, written by BOLT #12's authors and not by
  Faraday's. Its inputs are the vectors, random offers carrying every
  field, and offers with a byte changed, cut short, fields reordered and
  case mixed; both read the same fields or both refuse, and Faraday's
  stricter refusals above are listed as differences. Core Lightning
  checks a point through libsecp256k1, the app's own curve library, so
  the area judges each point's validity with btclib too, as its BOLT #11
  check does. rust-lightning is not taken as a judge: it reads through
  the `bech32` crate and rust-secp256k1, the app's, and
  `docs/ESTABLISHED.md` §9.5 already lists the `lightning` crate as
  colliding.
- **A parser of untrusted input, fuzzed:** the offer reader has a fuzz
  target in `faraday/fuzz`, with the published vectors as seeds, and is
  in `tools/fuzz-parsers.toml`.
- **Where it lives:** `core/faraday-codec`, beside `bolt11.rs`, holding
  no secrets; the view in the invoice area of `faraday-core`, its
  strings in `faraday-strings`. STANDARDS lists BOLT 12 now; its §5 row
  and its §6 lines come with the reader. Learn's page on Lightning
  invoices gains a section on offers with the reader.
- **Faraday Starter does not read offers.**
