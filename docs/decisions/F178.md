# F178 Established implementations first: Faraday writes its own only where none exists or none fits its key rules, and says why (2026-10-30)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Amended by F180
**Area:** crypto
**Held by:** crypto.established

*Amended by F180 (2026-10-31): HKDF and the BIP-39 core stay Faraday's own, so the trust entries for hkdf and for the bip39 crate's publisher are not written; getrandom is trusted, lzma-rust2 and rqrr are used as not established, and a FAT32 formatter is written here.*

**Why.** A review of 0.1.9 (2026-10-30) found that Faraday writes
pieces of cryptography an established library already offers (BLAKE2b,
HKDF twice, the AES round inside AEZ, the BIP-39 mnemonic core, X.509 and
DER, the single-key and multisig finalizer, an RSA generator adapter),
that the reasons recorded for writing them were size, a single caller or
a library's crate count, and that three of the pieces let a secret's
value steer their timing: the scalar arithmetic MuSig2, BIP-374 and
BIP-352 use on secrets, AEZ's AES round, and SLIP-39's interpolation.
The first of these contradicts what its code says of itself.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-30; `docs/ESTABLISHED.md`).

- Faraday uses an established implementation wherever one exists for
  what it needs: the standard's own reference implementation, or the
  implementation the ecosystem relies on, maintained and published by a
  project F71 trusts. Established means, mechanically, a trust entry in
  `supply-chain/audits.toml` or an imported audit covering the locked
  version (`docs/ESTABLISHED.md` §1.2).
- Faraday writes its own only where no established implementation
  exists or none fits its rules for keys. Each piece it writes gives one
  reason from a closed list: Faraday's own specification; no established
  implementation; one exists but does not fit the key rules (saying
  which); only the part used is written. A new piece written here, or a
  library that is not established, is a decision.
- The pieces written here today, as `docs/ESTABLISHED.md` §3 lists them
  once its work is built, are accepted under this decision.
- What is true to say, on the website and elsewhere: Faraday designs no
  cryptographic primitive of its own; the list of what it implements
  itself is generated from the manifest.
- Trust entries, each with its `docs/deps/` page: `hkdf`, `aes` and
  `x509-cert` from RustCrypto, `cms` when its 0.3.0 is released, and the
  publisher of the `bip39` crate (F71 amended).
- OpenPGP: Faraday keeps its own packets, only the part used; neither
  Sequoia nor rPGP is taken.
- scrypt: Faraday keeps its own, because the crate frees its
  passphrase-derived buffers unwiped; a change that wipes them is
  proposed upstream, and the switch is checked again when it is
  released.
- MuSig2: the switch to libsecp256k1's waits for the move to bitcoin
  0.33 and its secp256k1; Faraday does not carry two versions of
  secp256k1. The timing finding is fixed now, by moving the scalar
  arithmetic on secrets to libsecp256k1.
- Order: the timing fixes first, then the switches, then the rule in
  STANDARDS §0 and its enforcement.
