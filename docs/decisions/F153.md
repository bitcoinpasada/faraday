# F153 No kernel patch of Faraday's own on the Pi, and a new USB touchscreen approves itself by tapping a target: nothing is trusted by name (2026-10-28)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** security
**Held by:** tests

*Amends F145 (2026-10-28): a USB touchscreen named by the screen's profile is no longer trusted input. Amends F150 (2026-10-28): the HDMI screen's image takes its USB touch on these terms, and its Compatible kernel is the board's source as it is.*

**Why.** The Pi's Compatible build carried two changes of Faraday's own
to the kernel source, one taking V3D and its render node out of vc4 and
one taking DRM's `select KCMP` out, so that `common/kernel.forbidden`
could keep `KCMP` out of every image. A patch to the kernel is code
Faraday writes and must carry forward through every kernel update,
against a driver nobody else runs that way; what the two patches bought
is had by other means. A render node that only root can open is no
reach for the app, and `kcmp()` answers only for processes that pass
ptrace's read check against the caller. On the other side, a name is
what a USB device says about itself: any device can call itself by the
name an HDMI panel's touch gives, so trusting the first USB touchscreen
of that name trusted whatever was plugged in first. A touchscreen can
prove a person is at the screen the way a keyboard does, by doing what
only someone who sees the screen can.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-28; `docs/IMAGES.md` §2, §3,
`docs/SECURITY-MODEL.md` "What the kernel can reach").

1. **No kernel patch of Faraday's own.** The Pi's Compatible build
   compiles the board's kernel source as it is; its `linux-patches/`
   and the `buildroot.fragment` that applied them are gone. (The PC's
   `linux-patches/` are commits taken from mainline, F23, not changes of
   Faraday's own.)
   - **V3D stays, its render node root's alone.** vc4 binds the Pi 3's
     V3D and creates `/dev/dri/renderD128` beside `/dev/dri/card0`;
     `faraday/image/common/rootfs-overlay/etc/mdev.conf` gives both to
     root at 0600. The app runs as its own user and opens `/dev/fb0`;
     the only root processes after boot, init and its two shell loops,
     open no DRM node.
   - **`KCMP` is allowed in the Compatible kernel only.** It moves from
     `faraday/image/common/kernel.forbidden` to the Strict Pi's
     (`boards/pi3/builds/strict/kernel.forbidden`) and the PC's
     (`boards/x86_64-uefi/kernel.forbidden`), which keep forbidding it.
2. **A new USB touchscreen approves itself; nothing is trusted by
   name.** A touchscreen on USB is held back as every plugged-in device
   is, whether or not other input is trusted, and whatever it calls
   itself. The app draws a target at a place drawn from the session's
   seed (the system's randomness), on a sheet that covers the screen;
   its tap on the target lets it in. Taps beside the target count as
   wrong tries, and the third keeps it out until it is unplugged and
   counts toward the three devices kept out after which new keyboards
   and touchscreens are ignored until Faraday starts again
   (`faraday/faraday-core/src/inputs.rs`). Its tap on the sheet's
   Ignore keeps it out. A tap while another sheet is up, or before the
   randomness arrived, counts for nothing. A person may also press the
   target with input already trusted, as **Use this pointer** lets a
   pointer in. A touchscreen on a bus that is part of the machine (the
   2.8-inch's Goodix on I²C, a laptop's built-in touchscreen) stays
   trusted, and `--touch-name` picks it out on that bus only
   (`faraday/shells/stick/src/evdev.rs`). The HDMI panel's
   `panel.conf` gives no `TOUCH_NAME`.

**Amends** F145 (the USB touchscreen named by the screen's profile,
trusted input) and F150 (the HDMI screen's image: its USB touch and its
kernel).
