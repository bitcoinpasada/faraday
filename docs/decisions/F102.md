# F102 A late seed is said only at a press that needs it; the Pi's generator is mixed, not credited (2026-10-23)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** security

**Why.** The session's seed is the shell's first read of `/dev/random`,
which waits until the kernel counts its pool as ready. Home says the
system gave no randomness two seconds after the first display
(`SEED_GRACE_MS`). A kernel that credits no chip's generator can take
longer than two seconds on a slow board, and nothing a person does in
the first seconds needs randomness, so the line would tell them of a
fault that is not one. On the Pi the kernel credits bcm2835-rng in
full, so the pool's readiness rests on one chip maker's word, which F86
rules out on the stick.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-23). Amends F86.

- Nothing is said about randomness at start. Home's missing-seed line
  goes.
- A press that needs randomness while the seed has not arrived waits
  for it: its button reads "Waiting for randomness" and the action
  goes on by itself when the seed arrives, with no second press.
- A system that gives no randomness is still caught: a seed still
  missing a minute after the first display is a system that gives
  none, and a press that needs it then says so and does nothing.
- The Pi's bcm2835-rng is mixed into the pool and not credited, as the
  stick's RDRAND and firmware seed are. How is read from the Pi's
  kernel: where a quality of 0 also stops the kernel feeding the
  generator in, the shell writes the generator's bytes into
  `/dev/random`, which mixes them without crediting them. The wait on a
  real Pi is read off a dev Pi's log in passing.
