# F101 A window is laid out at its screen's own density; F78 is superseded (2026-10-23)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Amended by F100
**Area:** design
**Held by:** tests

**Why.** The wide layout was a 1280 × 800 page fitted to the window,
growing no more than a quarter beyond the screen's density, and F78
added a floor under it: a window the fit drew below three quarters of
its density took the one-column layout. A monitor got the 1280 page
larger rather than more room, and on a scale-2 screen a window just
over the floor drew its text at about half the size the compositor
asked for. F92 lays every window out at its own width, never scaled to
fit (`docs/LAYOUT.md` §7, batch L1).

**Decision** (owner, F92; batch L1, 2026-10-23).

- A window is laid out at its screen's density: the desktop app at the
  compositor's scale, the stick at the density its kernel command line
  gives (`faraday.dpi`, 160 by default, one pixel a unit, doubled on a
  panel 2800 pixels wide or more), the Pi panel at its 286 dpi. The
  person's Display scale multiplies it. The fit to 1280 × 800 and F78's
  floor go; `tokens::WIDE_FLOOR_DENSITY` is no longer read.
- The layout follows the window's class (`Faraday::window()`, LAYOUT
  §2), counted at that density times the Display scale. Until batch L2
  built the navigation of LAYOUT §3, compact, medium and expanded
  windows (under 1200 dp) took the one-column layout with Home as the
  menu, and large and extra-large ones the wide layout at their true
  width; that interim differed from LAYOUT §3 and was temporary. Since
  L2 the rail stands at medium and expanded (and on any low window),
  beside one-column pages, because the wide pages overflow an expanded
  window's page (renders at 960 × 640 dp: Stick visit's buttons leave
  their card, the wallet card's rows overlap). The wide layout's
  1600-unit maximum, centred, stays until the panes of LAYOUT §5.
- Held inside the Starter, the screens take the one-column layout in a
  window under 1280 dp, as before (`tokens::WINDOW_HELD_WIDE`).
- The cost: a window's units are its screen's, so a 1366 × 768 laptop
  at 160 dpi lays out 768 units tall where the fit gave it 800, and a
  window between 600 and 1199 dp that the fit drew wide took one
  column until L2 brought the rail; it now takes the rail beside one
  column.
