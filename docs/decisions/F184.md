# F184 Work over a tenth of a second on a Pi 3B+ runs on one worker in the core; shorter work is drawn first and done on the next tick (2026-11-02)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** speed
**Held by:** speed.worker-line

**Why.** Every costly press runs on the main thread today, the tick
after the frame that shows it (`docs/SPEED.md` §9 point 2). For a vault
on a Pi that is seconds of a still screen, a scanned PSBT's last part
holds the Scan sheet while it is checked, and the panic chord held
meanwhile does nothing until the work ends (`docs/WORKERS.md` §1).
Moving every costly press to a thread of its own would fix that, but
most of them are short: a BIP-39 seed, a SLIP-39 set or one picture
is over before a person would see the screen stand still, and each
would carry the worker's machinery (a job, its answer, its busy screen,
key material across a thread) for nothing a person notices.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-11-02). It amends `docs/SPEED.md` §9 point 2,
which now holds for short work alone.

1. **The line.** A press whose work takes longer than a tenth of a
   second, 100 ms, on a Raspberry Pi 3B+ at its full clock (1400 MHz)
   is a job and runs on one worker in the core (`docs/WORKERS.md` §3).
   Shorter work is drawn first and done on the tick after, as SPEED §9
   point 2 says. Work that grows with what it is given is judged at a
   large ordinary case (`docs/WORKERS.md` §2.1).
2. **A vault's key stretch is a job** whatever it measures.
3. **Until a Pi measures it**, a release build on a desktop PC stands
   in at a tenth of the line, since a Pi 3 is about ten times slower
   (`docs/history/SPEED-LOG.md`, "§9 Batch S2"). The list of jobs and
   presses drawn first is `docs/WORKERS.md` §2.1, made that way; the
   dev card's `work` rows on a Pi 3B+ confirm an item or move it.
4. **Key material crosses to the worker only for a job**, inside
   faraday-keys' own types (`docs/WORKERS.md` §6; F70 stands).
5. **A new costly press, or a change to `slow`'s list**
   (`faraday/faraday-core/src/slow.rs`), says which side of the line the
   press is on and how that was measured.

**What it costs.** Two paths stay for costly work, the tick after and
the worker, where one would do; and a stand-in for a Pi is an estimate
until a Pi dev card's rows are read, so an item near the line may move.
