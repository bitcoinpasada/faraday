# F125 Explore shows a key's secrets behind eyes (2026-10-26)

**Kind:** Record
**Status:** Active
**Area:** keys
**Held by:** review

**Why.** A person holding seed words or codex32 strings on paper, with
several keys loaded, cannot tell which key is which by fingerprint
alone. Explore showed only a key's public side, and the private keys a
wallet asks for when moving funds (an extended private key, an
address's WIF) could be seen nowhere.

**Decision** (owner, 2026-10-26; `docs/KEYS.md` §2, §3, DESIGN §5
Explore). Explore, opened from a key's row in the key tray, shows
everything derived from the key, on the stick and on mainnet too. Each
secret is behind its own eye, one press to show and one to hide, and
every eye is closed when Explore opens. The eyes cover the key's own
secret (its words and passphrase, or for a key with no words its master
seed in hex, with its codex32 secret and the strings of its set this
session holds), the root's and the account's extended private keys, and
each address's private key as WIF.

F70's closed list of reveals grows from eleven to fourteen:
`Reveal::SeedShown`, `Reveal::ExtendedPrivateKey` and
`Reveal::AddressKey`, each given only to Explore through its own method
in faraday-keys. A reason gives only its own text, so none of the three
lends the words or the passphrase. Each value is made once at the eye's
press, wiped when dropped, and kept on Explore's state only while it is
on screen; closing the eye, a change of key, path or page, leaving
Explore, the idle warning and a lock drop it, and drawing never derives
it.

This amends F70.
